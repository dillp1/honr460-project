# The 5A's of the Industrial Roundtable

_September 18, 2026_

Purdue's Industrial Roundtable (IR) is one of, if not the, largest career fair of the year.

At IR, a plethora of companies and students flock to Memorial Mall to network, make connections, snag free swag, and in rare cases, leave with a job offer.

IR, from my experience this year, greatly reflects the 5As framework in the field of creative problem solving.

The 5As go as follows:

- Actor
- Action
- Audience
- Artifact
- Affordances

---

In the scenario of IR, the **Actors** of this space are me, the rest of the student body, as well as the employees tabling for their company.

The **Action** mainly consisted of me interacting with recruiters at company booths.

This typically resulted in me handing the recruiter my resume, them skimming the resume, then directing me to a QR code to apply to their open positions online.

**Artifacts** that come out of IR can take many different shapes.

Some artifacts were materials, like the free company pens, stickers, business cards, etc.

Some were connections, such as learning names and faces of recruiters, or seeing a friend waiting in a grueling line.

And some were more informational, being learning about companies or job opportunities I had not previously known about.

Perhaps an **Audience** in the scenario could be the people waiting in line behind me while talking to a recruiter.

If you wanted to take it a step further, an audience member that did not directly participate in IR was the Purdue Exponent, with their reporting of IR in [this news article](https://www.purdueexponent.org/campus/students-flock-to-memorial-mall-for-industrial-roundtable/article_194db0b3-b64e-41da-a971-dd64a97e365c.html).

Finally, the heat and wait times were the largest **Affordance** of the event.

Despite IR being an amazing way to directly show interest and connect with a company, the heat of the sun was brutal, especially when trapped in an hour+ line to talk to your favorite company.

But to the credit of IR, it does allow for a large exchange of information, which makes it very easy for students of Purdue to know more about internship opportunities in order to boost their professional career.

---

Applying the 5As framework to IR directly impacts the amount of critical thinking that can happen both in the moment and in reflection.

It can often become easy to turn your brain off while in the moment, especially with something as stress-inducing as IR, but thinking critically on experiences such as this allows for more urgency in the task, as well as directly making IR a more productive experience.

In the moment, being able to think critically about the 5As as they relate to IR helped me be creative in my 30 second elevator pitch to the companies that I spoke with.

Being able to be creative with what lines I decided to stay in for the most benefit to myself was also influenced by the 5As framework, recognizing what IR was enabling for me (**affordances**), what **actors** I would need to interact with, as well as the **artifacts** I would be able to obtain.

Having reflected on IR using this framework, it has become more apparent just how impactful and critical IR has been to my week and for my future professional development.

Now that I am looking for employment after I graduate in May, this reflection made apparent to me the steps I took towards my future, and has made me feel more prepared for my job search.

If I were to attend a similar event in the future, I would arrive at IR earlier in the day to beat the heat, as well as research more companies that I am interested in working with.
